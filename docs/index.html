Treating Children Like Engineers, Not Passive Consumers

Why we completely rejected standard educational apps for our LKG son.

"He builds complex physical ramps from doormats and pillows to test gravity and velocity with his toy cars. He hated sitting still for adult-imposed flashcards, but loved mimicking us typing on our laptops. So we gave him a real wired keyboard, hooked it to our TV, and let him lead."

— Father & Creator of Nani Typing
🧠

Zero Over-Stimulation

No flashing stars, no cartoon fanfare, and no hyperactive sounds. A warm cream background, high-contrast navy lettering, and crisp phonetic feedback protect your child's natural attention span.

⌨️

Physical Sensory Feedback

Tapping glass screens is devoid of tactile resistance. A physical mechanical keyboard gives real physical feedback, developing fine-motor control, finger autonomy, and muscle memory.

🕊️

Child-Led Agency

No rigid levels to unlock. No timers or punishment for mistakes. He types freely for 2–3 minutes at a time, exploring phonics and language entirely on his own organic terms.

The 3 Progressive Levels

A developmental engine designed to evolve with your child from preschool through primary school.

🔤

Level 1: Phonics & Spelling Loop

Pressing any key speaks the uppercase letter name immediately. Hitting Space or Enter loops back through the word, visually magnifying each letter in vibrant orange as it is read, then pronounces the complete word.

📸

Level 2: Real-World Association

No clip art. When your child types a matching word, a real photograph of his actual surroundings (the family dog, his mother, the hens down the street) flashes for 2.8 seconds. Anchors written symbols directly to concrete reality.

🏎️

Level 3: Kinetic Physics Logic

Typing action words like FAST, SLOW, STOP, or DOWN triggers real physical kinetic movements—like an architectural wooden car speeding across or descending a gravitational ramp.

Zero Database.
Filename-as-the-Trigger.

Built for government schools, rural computer labs, and busy parents. Nani Typing has no complex configuration or databases.

  • Plug-and-Play USB: Simply drop your photos onto any USB thumb drive in a NaniTyping folder.
  • Universal Multilingual: Want to teach Telugu? Name the photo kodi.png. For English, name it hen.png. Works instantly in any language.
  • Hardware Accessibility: A $15 wired keyboard plugged into an existing household TV creates a Montessori computing terminal without buying a laptop.

Simple Directory Structure

USB_DRIVE /
└── NaniTyping /
    ├── kodi.webp   → Triggers on "KODI"
    ├── hen.png     → Triggers on "HEN"
    ├── mamma.jpg   → Triggers on "MAMMA"
    └── chair.webp  → Triggers on "CHAIR"

Case-insensitive matching behind the scenes ensures typing BAT, bat, or Bat matches effortlessly.
