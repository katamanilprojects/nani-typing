Parent & Teacher Setup Guide

Everything you need to set up a calm, tactile learning station in less than 5 minutes.

1. Installing Nani Typing on Your TV

You can easily install Nani Typing onto any Android TV, Google TV, or tablet:

  • Method A (Google Play Store): Once published, search for Nani Typing directly in the Google Play Store on your Android TV and click Install.
  • Method B (Direct Sideload via USB): Download the .apk from GitHub Releases, copy it to a USB drive, insert it into your TV, and install using any TV file manager.
  • Method C (Send Files to TV App): Install Send Files to TV on your phone and TV, send the APK file over your home Wi-Fi, and install.

2. The Hardware Setup (Low Cost & Accessible)

You do not need an expensive tablet or dedicated computer for your child. All you need is:

  • A Screen: Any Android TV, Google TV device (Chromecast, Xiaomi, Sony, OnePlus, TCL, etc.), or Android tablet.
  • A Physical Keyboard: Any standard USB wired keyboard or Bluetooth keyboard. Plug a wired keyboard directly into your TV's USB port.

Why wired? A standard wired keyboard has zero battery anxiety, zero Bluetooth pairing dropouts, and provides real physical key travel that gives young children tactile muscle memory.

3. The Card Launcher & Navigation

When you open the app, it presents two clean, high-contrast tactile cards:

  • Card 1: Level 1 (Free Typing) — Highlighted by default. Press Enter or Space to begin.
  • Card 2: Settings (Voice & Preferences) — Adjust speech pace, voice pitch, regional accent, and storage access.
  • Direct Typing Bypass for Young Children: If your child sits down and strikes any letter key directly on the menu, the app instantly enters Level 1 and types that letter. There is zero menu trapping!

4. Setting Up Real-World Photos (The Zero-DB Engine)

Nani Typing does not use cartoon clip art. Young children anchor language far more effectively when connected to their actual environment: real family members, their own pets, familiar household items, and street scenes.

Step-by-Step USB Setup:

  1. Insert any standard USB thumb drive into your computer or phone.
  2. Create a folder named: NaniTyping (Case-insensitive: nanityping, NANITYPING all work).
  3. Drop your photos and custom voice recordings into this folder. Name each file after the word you want to trigger:
    • kodi.webp → Displays photo when your child types KODI
    • amma.m4a / amma.mp3 → Plays mother's voice saying "Amma"
    • nanna.wav / nanna.m4a → Plays father's voice saying "Nanna"
    • hen.png → Displays photo when your child types HEN
  4. Subfolders & Audio Formats: You can organize files cleanly up to 4 levels deep (e.g. NaniTyping/Family/amma.jpg, NaniTyping/sounds/kodi.m4a, NaniTyping/voices/nanna.mp3). Supported audio formats: .m4a, .mp3, .wav, .ogg, .aac, .flac.
  5. Plug the USB drive into your Android TV and launch Nani Typing.
  6. Storage Permission: In Nani Typing, open the Settings card and select Storage / Media Access. Grant media access on the Android system screen, then press Back. Your media count will update immediately to show both indexed photos and voices!

5. Familiar Voice Recordings (Parent Voice Priority)

Children learn with profound joy and comfort when hearing the familiar voices of their parents and grandparents. Local audio files play directly from storage without internet buffering or streaming delays.

  • Direct Local Playback: Local audio recordings launch instantly via Android's local media player with no cloud dependency.
  • Parent Voice Priority: When a child types a word with a custom recording (e.g. AMMA), the engine spells the letters, and then plays your real recorded voice while the photo flashes.
  • Seamless Fallback: If a word has no custom recording (or if a file is unreadable), the app falls back to on-device speech synthesis smoothly.
  • Child Agency Priority: Striking any key on the keyboard immediately mutes ongoing audio, guaranteeing your child's typing speed is never blocked.

6. Universal Multilingual & Dialect Support

Because the trigger is simply the filename, Nani Typing has zero regional language barriers:

  • Telugu: Name a photo or voice recording kukka.jpg / kukka.m4a.
  • Hindi: Name the file kutta.jpg / kutta.mp3.
  • Tamil: Name the file naai.jpg / naai.wav.
  • English: Name the file dog.jpg / dog.m4a.

Matching is case-insensitive. Typing DOG, dog, or Dog instantly matches dog.jpg and dog.m4a.

7. Spacebar vs. Enter Key Mechanics

  • Letter Keys: Speaks uppercase letter names immediately upon pressing.
  • Spacebar (Word Spelling & Reading Loop): Spells completed word letter-by-letter with orange magnification ($1.5\times$), speaks whole word (or plays parent voice), flashes real photo with the word printed below, and adds a space.
  • Enter Key (Sentence Synthesis): Spells word (if mid-word), plays custom voice / TTS word, and then reads all words in the line together as a fluent sentence before moving to a new line.
  • Backspace (Del / Forward Del): Erases last letter and immediately mutes ongoing speech/voice.
  • Escape / Back: If text is present, resets canvas with *"Reset"*. If canvas is empty, returns to the Main Menu.

8. Kinetic Action Words Reference (Physics Play)

When your child types active verbs matching physical play, the TV screen simulates real physical dynamics with a minimalist tactile vehicle:

  • FAST, SPEED, RUN, ZOOM: Accelerates rapidly across the screen ($450\text{ms}$) with realistic linear velocity.
  • SLOW, CRAWL: Creeps across the canvas in a measured $3.5\text{s}$ crawl.
  • STOP, HALT, WAIT: Screeches to an immediate halt with braking suspension tilt and recoil.
  • DOWN, DROP, FALL: Renders a structural ramp incline and accelerates downward under simulated gravity.
  • UP, CLIMB: Powers uphill against gravitational resistance.
  • BOUNCE, HOP: Free-fall drop with elastic spring rebound and squash-and-stretch.
  • JUMP: Smooth parabolic upward leap and return to ground.

9. Tips for Parents: The Child-Led Flow

Do not force your child to sit for long sessions or complete structured flashcard drills. Allow him or her to type freely for 2 to 3 minutes whenever curiosity strikes. When children are free to lead their own exploration without adult interruption, their intrinsic joy of learning thrives naturally.
